Artemis II was a crewed lunar flyby mission that took place from April 1 to April 11, 2026.
NASA operated the Artemis II mission as the first crewed flight of the Space Launch System and Orion spacecraft.
The four-person crew included commander Reid Wiseman, pilot Victor Glover, mission specialist Christina Koch, and mission specialist Jeremy Hansen.
Reid Wiseman was the oldest person to travel beyond low Earth orbit and around the Moon during this mission.
Jeremy Hansen was the first non-American astronaut to travel around the Moon as part of the Canadian Space Agency crew.
Christina Koch was the first woman to travel beyond low Earth orbit and around the Moon on this mission.
Victor Glover was the first person of color to travel beyond low Earth orbit and around the Moon on this mission.
The Artemis II spacecraft, named Integrity by the crew, traveled a total distance of 700,237 miles during its nine-day mission.
Artemis II broke the Apollo 13 record for the greatest human distance from Earth, reaching 252,756 miles.
The mission launched from Kennedy Space Center's Launch Complex 39B on April 1, 2026, at 22:35:12 UTC.
Artemis II performed a closest approach of 4,067 miles to the far side of the Moon on April 6, 2026.
The crew observed a solar eclipse lasting 57 minutes while behind the Moon during their lunar flyby.
Artemis II was recovered by the USS John P. Murtha in the Pacific Ocean southwest of San Diego on April 11, 2026.
The mission included the AVATAR payload, which mimicked individual astronaut organs for the first time outside the International Space Station.
NASA tested optical communications using the Orion Artemis II Optical Communications System (O2O) with a downlink rate of up to 260 megabits per second.
The crew's zero-gravity indicator plush toy named Rise was designed by eight-year-old Lucas Ye and displayed during the mission.
