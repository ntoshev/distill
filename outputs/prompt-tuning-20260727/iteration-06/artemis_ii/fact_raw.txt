Artemis II was a crewed lunar flyby mission that took place from April 1 to April 11, 2026.
NASA operated the Artemis II mission as the first crewed flight of the Artemis program and the Space Launch System.
The four-person crew consisted of commander Reid Wiseman, pilot Victor Glover, mission specialist Christina Koch, and mission specialist Jeremy Hansen.
Victor Glover became the first person of color, Christina Koch the first woman, and Jeremy Hansen the first non-American to travel around the Moon.
Reid Wiseman was the oldest person to travel beyond low Earth orbit and around the Moon during this mission.
The spacecraft was named Integrity by the crew and was manufactured by Lockheed Martin for the Orion module and Airbus for the European Service Module.
The mission launched from Kennedy Space Center's Launch Complex 39B on April 1, 2026, at 22:35:12 UTC aboard an SLS rocket.
Artemis II traveled a total distance of 700,237 miles (1,126,922 km) during its nine-day duration.
The crew reached a maximum distance of 252,756 miles from Earth, breaking the previous record set by Apollo 13.
Orion performed a trans-lunar injection burn on flight day 2 using the European Service Module's AJ10 main engine.
The spacecraft flew closest to the Moon on April 6, 2026, at a distance of 4,067 miles (6,545 km) from the far-side surface.
The crew observed a solar eclipse lasting 57 minutes and photographed the solar corona and zodiacal light during the flyby.
The mission concluded with a splashdown in the Pacific Ocean southwest of San Diego on April 11, 2026.
The U.S. Navy recovered the crew aboard the amphibious transport dock USS John P. Murtha.
The mission included the AVATAR and ARCHeR payloads to study crew health and tissue analog responses in deep space.
NASA provided wake-up calls featuring music and greetings from former Apollo astronauts Charlie Duke and Jim Lovell.
