Artemis II, also known as Exploration Mission-2, was a crewed lunar flyby mission operated by NASA in 2026.
The mission utilized the Space Launch System rocket and the Orion spacecraft named Integrity by the crew.
Four astronauts comprising Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch, and Jeremy Hansen flew on the mission.
Reid Wiseman was the oldest person to travel beyond low Earth orbit and around the Moon during this flight.
Christina Koch was the first woman and Victor Glover was the first person of color to fly on a crewed lunar mission.
Jeremy Hansen was the first non-American astronaut to travel around the Moon as part of the Canadian Space Agency.
The mission launched on April 1, 2026, from Kennedy Space Center's Launch Complex 39B in Florida.
Artemis II reached a maximum distance of 252,756 miles from Earth, breaking the previous record set by Apollo 13.
The spacecraft performed a lunar flyby on April 6, 2026, passing 4,067 miles from the far side of the Moon.
The crew observed a solar eclipse and photographed the full Earth disk during their outbound flight.
Re-entry occurred on April 11, 2026, with the crew being recovered by the USS John P. Murtha in the Pacific Ocean.
The mission included the AVATAR and ARCHeR payloads to study human health effects in deep space.
Optical communications were tested using the Orion Artemis II Optical Communications System with a downlink rate of up to 260 megabits per second.
The mission featured a zero-gravity indicator plush toy named Rise, designed by eight-year-old Lucas Ye.
Public engagement during the mission led to the term "Moon joy" describing the intense happiness felt by the crew and observers.
The mission patch design incorporates a trajectory resembling the number 2 and depicts Earth from beyond the Moon's far side.
