The SpaceX initial public offering occurred on June 12, 2026, marking the largest IPO in history with a target valuation of US$1.77 trillion.
SpaceX was listed on the Nasdaq exchange under the ticker symbol SPCX with a subscription price of US$135 per share.
Elon Musk retained 82% to 85% of the voting power post-IPO through Class B shares that carry ten votes per share.
The company raised approximately US$86 billion, exceeding the initial target fundraising goal of US$75 billion.
Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley served as lead underwriters for the offering, while JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, and Citigroup acted as other underwriters.
Nasdaq changed its rules to allow SpaceX early inclusion in the Nasdaq-100 index just 15 trading days after the IPO.
SpaceX was ineligible for the S&P 500 index because it reported a net loss of US$4.94 billion in fiscal year 2025.
A contract with Anthropic for US$1.25 billion per month and an agreement with Google for US$920 million per month were cited as key evidence of AI revenue traction.
Underwriters denied subscription orders from investors in Hong Kong and China due to concerns over critical technology export controls and regulatory risks.
The IPO price of US$135 per share was set before investor roadshows in a move described as take-it-or-leave-it stock pricing.
On the first trading day, SpaceX shares closed at US$161, representing a 19% increase and a market capitalization of approximately US$2.1 trillion.
Ten days after listing, SpaceX launched a US$20 billion bond issue to repay borrowings and pay related fees and expenses.
Analysts at 23 underwriting banks issued 12-month price targets ranging from US$190 to US$800, with a median forecast of US$225.
Professor Aswath Damodaran modeled the launch, Starlink, and xAI segments to arrive at an enterprise value of approximately US$1.3 trillion.
The lock-up period for Elon Musk and other insiders was set at 366 days, while other pre-IPO investors faced an 180-day restriction.
