The 2026 FIFA World Cup was the 23rd edition of the tournament, jointly hosted by Canada, Mexico, and the United States.
Spain won the championship for the second time by defeating Argentina 1–0 in extra time during the final match.
Kylian Mbappé of France was the top scorer with 10 goals, becoming the all-time leading goalscorer in World Cup history.
Rodri of Spain was awarded the Golden Ball as the best player, while Unai Simón won the Golden Glove for best goalkeeper.
The tournament expanded to 48 teams from 32, featuring 104 matches played across 16 venues in 16 host cities.
New rules were introduced to reduce time-wasting, including 10-second substitution limits and 5-second visual countdowns for restarts.
Mandatory three-minute hydration breaks were implemented in every half, with the clock continuing to run during these pauses.
The draw for the tournament took place on December 5, 2025, at the Kennedy Center in Washington, D.C.
The opening match occurred on June 11, 2026, at Estadio Azteca in Mexico City, while the final was held on July 19 at MetLife Stadium.
Nearly 7 million people attended matches, setting a new record for the highest cumulative attendance in World Cup history.
The tournament generated a reported record revenue of $15 billion and distributed a total of $871 million in prize money.
The official mascots were Maple the Moose, Zayu the Jaguar, and Clutch the Bald Eagle, representing Canada, Mexico, and the United States respectively.
The match ball was named Adidas Trionda, featuring red, green, and blue colors representing the three host nations.
The official anthem was titled "DNA (More Than a Game)" by David Guetta, featuring Andrea Bocelli, Ejae, and Megan Thee Stallion.
The Netherlands received the Fair Play award for having the best disciplinary record among teams that reached the knockout stage.
