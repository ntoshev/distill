Artemis II was a crewed lunar flyby mission that took place from April 1 to April 11, 2026.
Artemis II's crew comprised four astronauts: Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch, and Jeremy Hansen.
Artemis II's spacecraft, named Integrity, was manufactured by Lockheed Martin for the Orion module and Airbus for the European Service Module.
Artemis II launched from Kennedy Space Center's Launch Complex 39B aboard a Space Launch System rocket on April 1, 2026.
Artemis II's closest approach to the Moon occurred on April 6, 2026, at a distance of 4,067 miles.
Artemis II's maximum distance from Earth reached 252,756 miles, surpassing the previous record set by Apollo 13.
Artemis II's crew observed a solar eclipse lasting 57 minutes while passing behind the Moon.
Artemis II's reentry utilized a steeper direct trajectory instead of a skip reentry to mitigate heat shield erosion risks.
Artemis II's crew was recovered by the USS John P. Murtha in the Pacific Ocean southwest of San Diego on April 11, 2026.
Artemis II's mission included the AVATAR payload, which mimicked individual astronaut organs for the first time outside the ISS.
Artemis II's optical communications system, O2O, transmitted data to Earth at a rate of up to 260 megabits per second.
Artemis II's zero-gravity indicator mascot, named Rise, was designed by eight-year-old Lucas Ye.
Artemis II's wake-up calls included songs such as "Sleepyhead" by Young & Sick and "Good Morning" by Mandisa and TobyMac.
Artemis II's commander, Reid Wiseman, was the oldest person to travel beyond low Earth orbit and around the Moon.
Artemis II's mission broke the record for the most people in deep space at once, with four astronauts aboard.
