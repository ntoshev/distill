Artemis II was a crewed lunar flyby mission that took place from April 1 to April 11, 2026.
Artemis II's crew comprised four astronauts: Commander Reid Wiseman, Pilot Victor Glover, Mission Specialist Christina Koch, and Mission Specialist Jeremy Hansen.
Artemis II's spacecraft, named Integrity, was manufactured by Lockheed Martin for the Orion module and Airbus for the European Service Module.
Artemis II launched from Kennedy Space Center's Launch Complex 39B aboard a Space Launch System rocket on April 1, 2026.
Artemis II's closest approach to the Moon occurred on April 6, 2026, at a distance of 4,067 miles (6,545 km).
Artemis II set a new record for the greatest human distance from Earth, reaching 252,756 miles (406,771 km) during its flyby.
Artemis II's crew broke the record for the most people in deep space at once, surpassing the Apollo 8 count of three.
Artemis II's reentry trajectory was modified to a steeper direct entry to mitigate risks from heat shield erosion observed after Artemis I.
Artemis II's crew was recovered by the USS John P. Murtha in the Pacific Ocean southwest of San Diego on April 11, 2026.
Artemis II's mission included the AVATAR payload, which mimicked individual astronaut organs for the first time outside the International Space Station.
Artemis II's optical communications system, O2O, demonstrated laser beam transmission to Earth with a downlink rate of up to 260 megabits per second.
Artemis II's zero-gravity indicator mascot, named Rise, was designed by eight-year-old Lucas Ye and flown inside the crew cabin.
Artemis II's wake-up calls included a Spotify playlist featuring songs like "Sleepyhead" by Young & Sick and "Good Morning" by Mandisa and TobyMac.
Artemis II's mission objectives were similar to those of Apollo 8, while its free-return trajectory resembled that flown by Apollo 13.
Artemis II's crew participated in President Donald Trump's America 250 speech on the National Mall on July 4, 2026.
